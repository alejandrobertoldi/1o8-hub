# Business Model Canvas

The output is the filled canvas: nine boxes with the venture's actual content, not
definitions of what each box means. The bonus that makes it useful: each box names
its riskiest assumption, because a canvas is a list of guesses until tested.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the venture in one sentence, who pays, and the current stage (idea,
   first customers, scaling).
2. Fill the nine boxes in this order (it forces customer-first thinking): Customer
   segments, Value propositions, Channels, Customer relationships, Revenue streams,
   Key resources, Key activities, Key partnerships, Cost structure.
3. Every box gets 2 to 4 entries specific to this venture. If an entry would fit
   any startup ("social media" as a channel), sharpen it until it would not.
4. Under each box, add "Riskiest assumption:" with the single belief that, if
   wrong, invalidates the box, and a cheap way to test it.
5. Close with the two assumptions to test first across the whole canvas.

## Worked example (two boxes, fictional)

Venture: SwapRack, peer-to-peer rental of camera gear between local photographers.

Customer segments: (a) hobbyist photographers who own idle gear worth 2,000+ GBP;
(b) event shooters needing occasional specialty lenses. Riskiest assumption: owners
will lend 1,500 GBP lenses to strangers for 25 GBP/day. Test: list 20 items via
manual concierge and count actual handovers.

Revenue streams: 15 percent commission per rental; deposit insurance upsell at 4
GBP/day. Riskiest assumption: renters accept a 15 percent markup versus rental
shops. Test: price 10 listings above and below shop rates, watch conversion.

## Output contract

Deliver all nine boxes as headed sections, each with 2 to 4 venture-specific
entries and a "Riskiest assumption:" line with its test. End with "Test first:"
naming the top two assumptions and their experiments. No generic filler entries;
all example figures fictional unless supplied.
